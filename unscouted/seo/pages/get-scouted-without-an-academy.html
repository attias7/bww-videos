How to Get Scouted Without an Academy: A Guide for Players 15–21

Not in an academy? This is the honest toolkit for getting seen: what scouts judge, where they actually watch, a football CV and email you can copy, what goes in your video, the scams to avoid and a 12-week plan.

Quick answer: To get scouted without an academy, play at the highest level you can hold a place in, go where scouts and coaches already look (non-league and open trials in the UK, college ID camps and club open tryouts in the US), and make yourself easy to judge with a one-page football CV, a short video and a polite email. Nobody can promise you a contract. What you control is how good your decisions, movement and attitude are when someone watches, and Unscouted's free soccer IQ test is a quick way to check the decision part.

Key takeaways

  • Scouts go where good players already are. Moving up a level does more for you than any highlight reel.
  • Scouts talk about ability and attitude. Decisions, off-ball movement and consistency are the parts you can train alone.
  • Make it easy: a one-page football CV, a 3 to 5 minute video plus one full half, and a short email with your next fixtures.
  • Real clubs don't ask you to pay a person for a trial, and they don't invite you by DM. Check everything with the club itself.
  • Measure yourself every 12 weeks. If your film doesn't change, change the plan, not the video.

Can you get scouted without an academy?

Yes, but it is rare, and the honest goal is "become good enough and visible enough that coaches look twice", not "get scouted". Players outside academies do get picked up through non-league football, college soccer and open trials. Your job is to keep improving and to keep playing where people with influence watch.

The numbers show how tight it is even one level below pro. According to the NCAA's 2024-25 figures, about 5.9% of US high school boys who play soccer go on to play NCAA college soccer, and only about 1.4% play at Division I (checked October 2026).

But narrow is not closed. Jamie Vardy is the famous English example: released by Sheffield Wednesday at 16, he went back to non-league football with Stocksbridge Park Steels, later moved to Fleetwood Town, and Leicester City paid £1 million for him before he won the Premier League in 2016. He's an outlier, not a plan. The useful lesson is that he kept playing at a level where he could be watched, and kept getting better.

Set your goal honestly. "Become the best player in my team, then get into a higher-level team" is a goal you can work on every week. A better club, a college place, a semi-pro contract or simply being a much better player are all real wins on the way. What do scouts look for in a player?

Scouts look for ability and attitude together. Ability is not just tricks: it's whether you make good decisions quickly, move well without the ball and play at the same level every week. Attitude is your body language, effort and how you treat teammates. You can train almost all of it without an academy coach.

Gwynn Williams, a long-time scout who worked at Chelsea, told FourFourTwo it's about "the two a's, ability and attitude, you have to have both", and said he judges ability partly by decisions, such as whether a player sees the forward pass. A small University of Chichester study that interviewed five Premier League and Championship scouts also picked out decision making and risk-taking as key.

What does a "scout moment" look like on the pitch?

Many of the moments scouts remember happen without the ball: the five seconds after you lose it, the run nobody passes to, the scan before a pass arrives. Here is one. You are the lime player in midfield, their goal is at the top. It's 0-0 after 34 minutes.

Your pass was cut out in their half. Their midfielder has just picked up the loose ball.

What do you do?

  • A Drop back into your midfield position.
  • B Press him now, from behind him. (pro pick)
  • C Go and mark their other midfielder.

Why B: he faces his own goal with the ball on his far side, and your striker and 10 stand in the lanes back to his center backs. He can't see you and his easy pass is gone, so close him fast, stay on your feet and make him rush it. Why not A or C: dropping off gives him time to turn, and your winger is already next to their other midfielder. A scout watching this sees a player who reacts to losing the ball, which is exactly the attitude they talk about.

Where do scouts actually watch players?

Scouts and recruiting coaches watch where good players are already concentrated: higher-level leagues, non-league and semi-pro games, college programs, and genuine open trials run by clubs. Grassroots and Sunday league games get far less attention. So the most useful thing you can do is move yourself into those places.

In the UK: non-league is the late developer's door

Below the four professional divisions, the National League System runs down through six steps of non-league football, with the National League at Step 1. It is competitive adult football, clubs higher up watch it, and players move up from it. Look for pre-season open trials at local non-league clubs, ask about their under-18s or reserves, and play up a step if you can hold your place. A regular starter at a higher step is more visible than a star at a lower one.

Big professional academies are much harder to walk into. Chelsea, for example, says on its website that its academy for male players does not hold open trials. That's normal for top clubs, so put your energy where doors are open.

In the US: college and club open tryouts

College soccer is the biggest late route. Besides NCAA Divisions I, II and III there are NAIA and junior college programs, so the range of levels is wide. SoccerWire's summary of NCAA rules says Division I and II coaches can't make recruiting contact until June 15 after your sophomore year, but you can email them and attend their camps before that. Rules change, so check the current NCAA calendar.

What if there's no good club near you?

Then the nearest higher level is your target, even if it means travel for training twice a week. If that isn't possible yet, play the most competitive adult football you can (a strong amateur or semi-pro side often beats a weak youth team), film it, and use camps and open trials a few times a year. Don't move cities on the strength of a promise from someone you met online. If you're under 18, any move or trip is a family decision.

How do you make a football CV?

A football CV (or player profile) is one page that lets a coach judge in 30 seconds whether to watch your video. It lists your details, positions, current team and level, a few honest facts and how to reach you. Keep it factual: coaches trust "regular starter, U18 regional league" more than "elite playmaker".

What should be in a highlight video for scouts?

A good scouting video is 3 to 5 minutes, starts with your best clips, makes you easy to spot and shows decisions, not just goals. Add one full unedited half as a separate link so a coach can see you weren't only good for five moments. Put your name, position, age and contact on the first screen.

  • Make yourself easy to find: freeze the first frame of each clip and circle or arrow yourself.
  • Show decisions: a scan then a forward pass, a run that pulls a defender away, a quick switch of play.
  • Show off-ball and defensive work: pressing, recovery runs, winning the ball back. These are rare in highlight videos, so they stand out.
  • Use recent games: clips from the last 12 months. Old clips make coaches wonder what happened since.
  • Film from high up if you can: a raised, wide angle shows your positioning, not just the ball.

Should you use social media to get scouted?

Social media is a place to host your video and show you're serious, not a scouting system. Keep one clean account with your position, team and an email in the bio, post real match clips rather than only skills, and keep it free of anything you wouldn't want a coach to see. Never treat a DM as a trial invitation: real clubs use official channels.

How do you contact clubs and coaches yourself?

Send a short, specific email to the right person: the first-team or youth manager at a non-league club, or the head or assistant coach at a college program. Say who you are, your position and level, why that club, and give your video, CV and next fixtures. One follow-up after about two weeks is fine; more than that is not.

Email template (edit every line for each club):

Subject: [Position], born [year], [current team and league] – video and next fixtures

Hi Coach [surname],

I'm [name], a [position] born in [year], playing for [team] in [league], where I've started [number] of [number] games this season. I'm interested in [club or college] because [one real reason: style of play, level, location].

Highlights (4 min): [link]. Full first half vs [opponent]: [link]. My one-page CV is attached.

My next games are [date, time, place] and [date, time, place]. My coach, [name], is happy to be contacted at [email].

Thank you for your time,
[Name] · [phone/email] · [parent's email if under 18]

Also ask your own coach to help. A call or email from a coach who has watched you every week counts for more than anything you write about yourself. And be realistic about where you send it: ten emails to clubs and colleges at your level beat a hundred to famous ones.

Do you need an agent to get scouted?

No. You don't need an agent to get noticed, and a real agent earns money when a deal happens, not by charging you up front. Under FIFA's Football Agent Regulations, agents can't approach a minor until six months before the player is able to sign a first professional contract, and a minor's representation agreement must be signed by a parent or guardian.

What is a good 12-week plan to get scout-ready?

A good 12-week plan has four blocks: get a baseline, fix your biggest weakness, add pressure, then prove the change on film and send it out. Run it alongside your normal team training. The aim is a clear before-and-after you can show, not more hours for the sake of it.

How do you scout yourself honestly?

Rate yourself after every match from 1 to 5 on the areas below, and write one piece of evidence from the game for each score. No evidence means no score. Be harsher than you want to be, because a scout will be. Over 12 weeks this gives you a clear picture of your level and proof you've improved.

FAQ

Can you get scouted if you're not in an academy?

Yes, but it's rare and nobody can promise it. Players outside academies get noticed through non-league football in the UK, college soccer and club open tryouts in the US. Play at the highest level you can and get better at decisions, movement and consistency.

Is 16, 17 or 18 too late to get scouted?

It's later than the academy path, but not too late for non-league, semi-pro or college football. Jamie Vardy was released at 16 and rebuilt his career in non-league. Use the time to improve and to play at a level where people watch.

Do scouts go to Sunday league or grassroots games?

Rarely, because scouts go where good players are concentrated. A higher youth league, non-league or college team gives you far more chances to be seen than a strong performance at a low level.

How do I contact a club for a trial?

Find the right coach's email on the club's official website and send a short message with your position, age, current team, a video link, a one-page CV and your next fixtures. Follow up once after about two weeks.

Can you get scouted on social media?

Social media can host your video, but it isn't how most clubs recruit. Keep one clean account with real match clips and an email in the bio, and never treat a DM as a real trial invitation.

Do I need an agent to get scouted?

No. Under FIFA's agent rules, agents can't approach minors until six months before they can sign a first professional contract, and a parent or guardian must sign a minor's representation agreement. Be careful with anyone who asks for an upfront fee.

Are paid football trials worth it?

Treat any paid trial that claims to be with a professional club as a likely scam until the club confirms it. Chelsea, for example, says it never asks for money for trials. Paid showcases are not always scams, but ask who will be watching and verify it.

What do football scouts look for?

Scouts talk about ability and attitude. Ability includes decision making, such as seeing the forward pass, plus movement and consistency. Attitude includes body language, effort and how you treat teammates.
