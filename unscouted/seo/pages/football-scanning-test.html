How does the scanning test work?

The test shows you a tactics board with you (the lime player), your teammates in white and their players in red, for a short time. Then every player except you disappears and you answer one question about the picture. It copies what happens in a match: you look away from the ball for a moment, and you have to remember what you saw.

  1. Pick how long you get to look. Easy is 3 seconds, Normal is 2 seconds, Hard is 1.2 seconds.
  2. Read your role. Each round starts with one line, for example "Your center back is passing to you."
  3. Look. The players appear and a bar counts down.
  4. Answer. The players vanish. Tap an answer, or tap a zone on the board.
  5. Check. The players come back so you can see what you missed, with one line on why it matters.

You play 10 rounds, picked at random from 12 situations, so two runs are rarely the same. The questions are the ones a coach shouts from the sideline: where's the free man, is someone on your back, which side is open, how much time do you have.

What is scanning in football?

Scanning means quick turns of the head, away from the ball, to see where opponents, teammates and space are before the ball reaches you. You take a picture, look back at the ball, and your first touch already knows where it is going. In soccer (the US name for the same game) coaches call it "checking your shoulder" or "having a look".

Scanning has two parts. The first is taking the look at the right moment, usually just before the pass to you is played and while it travels. The second is keeping what you saw. This test only trains the second part: how much one look gives you. For the first part, and for drills you can do alone with a ball and a wall, read our guide on how to scan in soccer.

What does your score mean?

Your score shows how much of the picture you kept after one look. 8 or more out of 10 means you took in the free player, the pressure and the space. A low score usually means you only saw the ball and the nearest player. Your average answer time matters too: a slow answer means you had to rebuild the picture.

Be honest with yourself about what this measures. A screen test can't see whether you turn your head in a match, how fast the game feels when you're tired, or what you do with the ball. It checks one useful thing: how much you keep from a short look.

How do you get a better score?

Look with a question in your head. Players who score well don't try to remember every dot. They check three things in order: is someone on me, where is the free teammate, and which side has space. That's also the order that decides your first touch in a game.

  • Check yourself first. Find the nearest red player to you and how far away he is.
  • Then find the free man. Look for a white player with no red player close to him.
  • Then the side. Notice which half of the board has fewer red players.
  • Say it in words. "Man on, free on the right." Words are easier to keep than a picture.
  • Train it with the ball. Use the wall-pass and shoulder-check drills in our scanning guide, so the look becomes a habit before every pass.

FAQ

What is a football scanning test?

A football scanning test checks how much of the pitch you take in from a quick look. This one shows a match picture for a few seconds, hides the players and asks a question about what you saw, like where the free teammate was.

Is this soccer scanning test free?

What is a good score on the scanning test?

8 or more out of 10 on Normal (2 seconds) is a strong result. Once you get there, try Hard mode, where you only get 1.2 seconds to look.

Does a scanning test show how well I scan in games?

Only partly. It shows how much you keep from one look. It can't see whether you turn your head in a real match, so pair it with scanning drills in training.

How can I improve my scanning?

Look with a question in mind: is someone on me, where is the free player, which side is open. Then build the habit in training with a shoulder check before every pass you receive, and name one thing you saw.
