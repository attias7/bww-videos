What is soccer IQ?

Forget the vague version ("he just reads the game"). Soccer IQ, or football IQ as most of the world calls it, is three things that happen in about a second:

  1. See: you know where the space, your teammates and the nearest opponents are before the ball reaches you.
  2. Decide: you pick the best of the two or three real options.
  3. Do: you play it early enough that the option is still open.

Most "bad decisions" are really a problem at step one. You didn't look, so you only found out about the free player after your first touch, when it was too late.

Here is a moment that shows all three. You're a left winger. Your left back plays it into your feet and their right back steps up towards you. What do you do?

  • A Take their full-back on down the line.
  • B Play it inside to your 10 between the lines. (pro pick)
  • C Play it back to your left back.

Why B: their two midfielders are level with you and nobody has stepped up to your 10. He's free in the gap in front of their center backs and can turn. Option A runs straight at a defender who is already goal-side with no room to build speed. Option C is safe and would be right if nothing were on, but here it throws away an open pass forward.

Notice the answer comes from a detail in the picture, not from a rule like "always go forward". That's the skill you're training: noticing the detail fast.

What are the parts of soccer IQ?

Soccer IQ breaks down into five parts you can train separately: scanning, anticipation, tactical understanding, reading the game state and decision speed. Most players are strong in one or two and weak in the rest, which is why a breakdown helps more than a single "high" or "low" label.

Why don't ball drills train soccer IQ?

Cone drills, juggling and wall passes are great for your touch. But in most of them the answer is fixed before you start. You know where the next cone is, so there is nothing to decide.

Decisions get better when you face a picture, choose, and then find out if you were right. That needs three things a cone drill doesn't have:

  • A changing picture: opponents and teammates in different spots each time.
  • A choice: more than one option that could work.
  • Feedback: someone or something that shows you the better answer and why.

There is some research support for practising with match footage. A 2022 systematic review in Frontiers in Human Neuroscience looked at 10 studies of video-based training with football players and found that 8 of them reported better anticipation or decision-making after the training. Be careful with that, though: the studies were small (300 players in total), the authors couldn't combine the results into one number, and only one study tested whether it carried over to real matches. So treat it as "promising, worth doing alongside playing", not as proof that screen work makes you a better player on its own.

Honest caveat: nothing replaces playing real games. Every method below works best as an extra on top of the matches and training you already do.

How do you watch film to train soccer IQ?

Watch like a player, not a fan: one player, one question, with pauses. Following the ball shows you what happened. Following one player in your position, and pausing before he receives, shows you why it happened, and that's the part you can copy.

  1. Pick one player in your position, from a full match or extended highlights, not a goals compilation.
  2. Pick one question for the whole half, like "where does he look before he gets the ball?" or "when does he run in behind?"
  3. Pause before he receives. Say what you'd do. Then play and compare.
  4. Write down three moments where his choice surprised you, and what he saw that you didn't.

How do academy players develop soccer IQ, and how can you copy it?

Academy players don't have a secret drill. They get more decision reps with feedback: small-sided games most days, coaches who stop play and ask questions, and video reviews of their own matches. Outside an academy you can copy each piece, it just takes a bit of organising.

How do you know your soccer IQ is improving?

Soccer IQ is hard to measure with a number, so don't trust anyone who promises a percentage. Instead, check these every month:

  • Do you know where your nearest teammate is before you receive, more often than before?
  • Are you losing the ball less from your first touch?
  • When you watch your own match clips, do you spot the free player sooner?
  • On scenario sets, are you choosing the pro option more often and faster, on situations you haven't seen before?
  • Do your coach and teammates ask you for the ball more often?

If the answers start moving, keep going. If nothing changes after a month, change one thing: usually it means you're getting reps but skipping the review part.

FAQ

Can soccer IQ be trained?

Yes. Seeing, deciding and acting in time are skills, and skills improve with reps and feedback. You need practice where the picture changes, you have to choose, and you find out the better answer. Playing games, watching pros in your position and scenario training all give you that.

How long does it take to improve soccer IQ?

There is no fixed timeline and anyone who gives you one is guessing. Give a routine at least a month of steady practice before you judge it, and judge it with the checklist above rather than a feeling.

What soccer IQ drills can I do alone?

Look over both shoulders before every pass you receive from a wall, and say what you saw. Do 10 scenario questions a day with a time limit. Watch one half of a pro game following only the player in your position. Review three of your own on-ball moments after every match.

Is soccer IQ more important than skill?

You need both. Great technique with poor decisions means you do the wrong thing well. Good decisions with poor technique means you see the right pass and can't play it. For most players outside academies, decisions are the part that gets the least practice, so that's often where the quickest gains are.

Are soccer IQ apps worth it?

They can be, if you use them regularly and they give you clear feedback. Check the price model first: some are monthly subscriptions, some need a VR headset, and some are a one-off payment. Try any free trial or free test before you pay.

At what age should you start soccer IQ training?

There's no minimum age, because any game where you have to choose trains it. Small-sided games and simple questions like "what did you see?" work for young players. Structured work like film study, timed scenarios and self-review suits players from their early teens, including players aged 15 to 21 who never had academy coaching.

What are the best soccer IQ drills?

The best ones make you look, choose and get feedback: rondos with a touch limit, 3-zone small-sided games, a wall pass where a friend shows fingers behind you, pausing pro matches to predict the next pass, and timed match scenarios with the pro answer explained.

How do pros improve their soccer IQ?

Mostly through thousands of decision reps with feedback: small-sided games and rondos in training, coaches who stop play to ask what they saw, and video review of their own matches. You can copy each of these outside an academy with friends, a phone camera and pro match footage.

Can you test your soccer IQ?

Yes, partly. A scenario test shows whether you recognise the best option in real match pictures under a time limit, and a scanning test shows how much you keep from one look. Neither sees how you play under pressure, so use them to find what to train, not as a final grade.
