What is Train Effective?

Train Effective is a soccer training app (called a football app in the UK store) that works like a digital academy: drills, workouts and programs for technique, tactics, fitness and mentality, built by UEFA-licensed coaches and Premier League experts, with an AI coach that recommends sessions. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Android.

From its official listings:

  • Drill library. Over 150 drills, training sessions, workouts and programs, with new content added monthly, plus a planner to schedule and track training through the year.
  • AI coach. The US App Store subtitle is "Training Plans & AI Coach", and recent release notes describe CoachAI giving recommendations tailored to your goals.
  • Game Brain. The listing calls it "our performance analysis training tool", built to give players a step-by-step understanding of pro players' decisions and tactics, using Premier League analysts.
  • Mentality sessions with pro athletes, including Rio Ferdinand, on self-belief and motivation.
  • Academy plan extras on its website: 1-on-1 mentorship and live online masterclasses.

Ratings (checked October 2026): 4.8 stars from 13K ratings on the US App Store and 4.8 from 7.2k on the UK App Store. On Google Play it lists 1M+ downloads and 30,000+ reviews. That's a lot of satisfied users. If you want one complete training app, it's a serious option.

Watching a decision vs making one

Train Effective's Game Brain teaches how professionals decide: an analyst breaks down what a pro did and why. A decision trainer flips the order: you see the picture first, commit to an answer against a clock, then learn what you missed. Both are useful, but they train different things.

Knowing why someone else made a choice is different from making it yourself, with a defender closing and a second to spare. Coaches often call this the gap between knowing and doing. A dedicated decision trainer puts you in the moment first:

  • you see the picture before anyone explains it,
  • you commit to an answer, against a clock,
  • then you find out if you were right, and which detail you missed.

That order matters: you find out what you notice on your own, not what seems obvious once someone has pointed at it.

There is some research behind training decisions from video and pictures. A 2022 systematic review of 10 studies on video-based training with soccer players found that in 8 of them, players scored better on anticipation or decision-making tests after training. The studies were small, the authors couldn't pool the results, and only one tested whether it carried over to the field. Encouraging, not proof.

Try one: a midfielder under pressure

Here's what "making the call yourself" looks like. Give yourself 15 seconds before you read the options.

  • A Turn and drive forward into midfield.
  • B Play it first time out to your left back. (pro pick)
  • C Lay it straight back to your center back.

Why B: look at their winger. He has left your left back to chase your center back, so your left back is alone on the touchline with their full-back about 20 metres away. Play it first time, before their midfielder gets tight, and your team is out of the press.

Why not A: their midfielder is about 4 metres behind you and coming. Try to turn and he gets a foot in, and you lose it in the middle of your own half.

Why not C: laying it back keeps the ball, and it's the right call when your center back has time. Here their winger is already closing him, so you hand the problem straight back.

The lesson isn't "always go wide". The answer was in the picture: one opponent leaving his man. Seeing that cue fast, many times, for your position, is what scenario training is for. Our guide to decision making walks through three more.

Is there a free Train Effective alternative?

Yes, for most of what it does. Free YouTube drill sessions and a written plan cover technique and fitness, small-sided games train decisions under real pressure, and watching matches with a focus on one player in your position builds game understanding. Paid apps add structure and feedback, not secrets.

  • Technique: 20 minutes of wall passes and ball mastery, 4 or 5 days a week. Our soccer training plan lays out a week.
  • Decisions: 3v3 or 4v4 with friends, two-touch limit. Small spaces force fast choices.
  • Game understanding: watch one player in your position for 15 minutes of a match and note every time he checks his shoulder and what he does next. Our guide to analyzing a match as a player shows how.
  • Tests: the free soccer IQ test and scanning test show where you stand.

How do you use Train Effective and a decision trainer together?

Use Train Effective (or any drills app) for your technical and fitness sessions, and add 5 to 10 minutes of decision training on most days. They don't compete: one builds the technique, the other trains the choices you make with it. A simple week for a player training on their own looks like this.
