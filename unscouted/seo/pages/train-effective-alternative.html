What Train Effective is (and does well)

Train Effective calls itself a digital soccer academy (or football academy, depending on which store you open). Its app store listing describes training across four areas: technique, tactical knowledge, fitness and mentality. From the official listings and website:

  • A big drill library. Over 150 drills, sessions, workouts and programs, with new content added monthly, and a schedule tracker to plan your week.
  • Mindset sessions with pro athletes and experts, including Rio Ferdinand, on self-belief, motivation and discipline.
  • Game Brain. The Google Play listing calls it "our performance analysis training tool", built to give you a step-by-step understanding of pro players' decisions, tactics and strategies, using Premier League analysts. The pricing page lists it as "tactical analyses from Premier League analysts".
  • Academy plan extras: 1-on-1 mentorship from coaches and weekly live online masterclasses.

Prices (website): PRO is $13 a month billed annually, Academy is $33 a month billed annually, and Clubs is $50 per player per year. There's a 7-day free trial. The US App Store lists a range of in-app prices for the same plans, from $12.99 and $19.99 a month up to $399.99 for 12 months of Academy, so the price you see may depend on where you sign up.

Ratings: on Google Play it showed 1M+ downloads, about 4.5 stars from over 30,000 reviews, and an update on September 10, 2026. On the UK App Store it showed 4.8 stars from 7.2k ratings. That's a lot of happy users. If you want a complete training app, it's a serious option.

Watching a decision vs making one

Here's the honest difference. From its own description, Game Brain teaches you how professionals decide: an analyst breaks down what a pro did and why. That's useful. Understanding why a pro chose the switch instead of the forward pass is real knowledge.

But knowing why someone else made a choice is different from making the choice yourself, with a defender closing and a second to spare. Coaches often call this the gap between knowing and doing. A dedicated decision trainer puts you in the moment first:

  • you see the picture before anyone explains it,
  • you commit to an answer, against a clock,
  • then you find out if you were right, and which detail you missed.

That order matters: you find out what you actually notice on your own, not what seems obvious once someone has pointed at it. Neither approach is "better". They're different tools. Train Effective's Game Brain is part of a bigger app. A decision trainer does only this one job.

There is some research behind training decisions from video and pictures: a 2022 systematic review of 10 studies on video-based training with soccer players found that in 8 of them, players scored better on anticipation or decision-making tests after training. The studies were small, the authors couldn't pool the results, and only one tested whether it carried over to the field. Encouraging, not proof.

Try one: a midfielder under pressure

Here's what "making the call yourself" looks like. Give yourself 15 seconds before you read the options.

  • A Turn and drive forward into midfield.
  • B Play it first time out to your left back. (pro pick)
  • C Lay it straight back to your center back.

Why B: look at their winger. He has left your left back to chase your center back, so your left back is alone on the touchline with their full-back about 20 metres away. Play it first time, before their midfielder gets tight, and your team is out of the press.

Why not A: their midfielder is about 4 metres behind you and coming. Try to turn and he gets a foot in, and you lose it in the middle of your own half.

Why not C: laying it back keeps the ball, and it's the right call when your center back has time. Here their winger is already closing him, so you hand the problem straight back.

The lesson isn't "always go wide". It's that the answer was in the picture: one opponent leaving his man. Seeing that cue fast, many times, for your position, is what scenario training is for. Our guide to decision making walks through three more.

Using both

Train Effective and a decision trainer don't really compete. One builds your technique and fitness, the other trains the choices you make with that technique. A simple week:

  • 3 days: a Train Effective drill or workout, or your own technical session.
  • Most days, 5 to 10 minutes: 5 to 10 situations for your position, deciding before you read the options.
  • Weekend: play, then write down one good and one bad decision from the match and what you saw (or didn't) before it.
