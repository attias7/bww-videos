What VR does well

It is worth being fair here, because VR does some things no picture on a screen can.

  • Real scanning movement. In a headset you have to physically turn your head to see what is behind you, the same way you do on the pitch. That builds the habit of checking your shoulder, not just the knowledge that you should.
  • Pictures that move. Players run, the ball travels, the space closes. You have to decide in time, not after studying a still image.
  • Lots of reps without tired legs. Be Your Best says pros use it to train scanning and decisions without extra physical load. That also suits a young player who already trains most days.
  • Feedback and tracking. Be Your Best's personal plan includes replay analysis and stat tracking, so you can see whether your scanning numbers move.

Be Your Best's website also cites a 28% average increase in scan rate from a study with a German youth national team over 9 weeks. We haven't read that study, so we're reporting it as their claim, not as a checked fact.

Research on VR in soccer is still early. For example, a small 2023 study in the journal Virtual Reality found "preliminary support" for VR heading practice, but the authors list clear limits: short training, no ball contact, and no group practising with a real ball for comparison. That is not a reason to avoid VR. It is a reason to be careful with big claims, from anyone.

What you can train without VR

Most bad decisions in soccer have two parts: you didn't see something, or you saw it and didn't know what it meant. VR is strongest on the first part. The second part, understanding the picture, can be trained with nothing more than a phone.

Scenario training works like this. You see a match moment on a tactics board: where you are, where your teammates and opponents are, where the ball is. You get three realistic options. You pick one, then see the option a good coach would choose and the detail in the picture that decides it. For example: you are a winger receiving on the touchline, and whether you take on the full-back depends on how far away his covering center back is. Over a few dozen situations you start to recognize the cues that matter for your position.

There is some research behind training decisions from video and pictures. A 2022 systematic review of 10 studies on video-based training with soccer players found that in 8 of them, the trained players did better on anticipation or decision-making tests. The authors couldn't pool the results in a meta-analysis, so it is encouraging rather than conclusive.

You can also train the physical scanning habit without a headset:

  • Wall passes with a look: before every return pass, check over one shoulder and name something you see.
  • Watch games with a pause: follow one player in your position, pause before he receives, and say what you would do. Our guide to analyzing a match as a player explains how.
  • In training: make one target per session, such as "two looks before every first touch".

Using both

VR and scenario training are not really rivals. They train different halves of the same skill. A simple week might look like this:

  • At home (if you have VR): 2 or 3 short scanning sessions in the headset.
  • On the bus or between classes: 5 situations on your phone, deciding before you read the options.
  • At the weekend: play, then write down one good and one bad decision from the match.

FAQ

Does VR soccer training work?

VR is good at training scanning with real head movement and moving pictures. Research on it in soccer is still early and mostly small studies, so be careful with big claims. The most honest answer is that it can help, especially alongside real games, and the best way to know is to try it with a money-back guarantee.

How much is Be Your Best?

On its website the personal plan is $19 USD a month billed annually, or $29 USD a month billed monthly, with 3 seats included. Teams and clubs book a call for pricing. No free trial is mentioned, but there is a 14-day money-back guarantee. Prices checked October 2026.

Which headset do you need for VR soccer training?

Be Your Best's VR app is on the Meta Quest store. Its FAQ also lists Oculus Quest 1 and 2 and Pico Neo 2 headsets, but that list may be out of date, so check with them for your exact headset.

Is there VR soccer training on phone?

Not true VR. Be Your Best has a companion phone app for progress, leaderboards and session review, but the VR training itself needs a headset. On a phone you can do scenario training instead, which trains reading the picture and choosing options.

Can you train scanning without VR?

Yes. Do wall passes where you check over your shoulder before every return pass, set yourself a "two looks before every first touch" target in training, and watch games pausing before your position's player receives the ball. Scenario training then helps you understand what you saw.
