What does number 10 mean in soccer?

In soccer, "number 10" means the attacking midfielder: the player who sits behind the striker, between the opponent's midfield and back line, and turns possession into chances. It is also the shirt number that this player traditionally wears, which is why both meanings get mixed up. On the field, the position matters more than the shirt.

You will hear other names for the same job: playmaker, attacking midfielder, "the hole", the enganche (Argentina and Uruguay) and the trequartista (Italy, roughly "three-quarter player"). In football (what most of the world calls soccer), the role has the same reputation everywhere: the 10 is the team's creative player, the one who is expected to make something happen.

A quick way to see it: the 6 protects, the 8 runs box to box, the 9 scores, and the 10 creates, mostly from the space just outside the opponent's box.

Where does the number 10 come from?

The number 10 comes from early shirt numbering, where numbers matched positions. In the 2-3-5 formation, 10 was the inside left forward. As formations changed, that player dropped into the space behind the striker, and the shirt went with him.

What are the versions of the number 10 role?

There are five common versions of the number 10: the classic playmaker, the trequartista, the shadow striker, the free 8 in a 4-3-3, and the false 9 who drops into the 10 space. They share one area but do different jobs, so first find out which version your coach wants.

Is a number 10 a midfielder or a forward? By position, a midfielder: the 10 is the most advanced of the midfield. By job, half a forward: most of what a 10 is judged on happens in the final third. If your team plays a 4-3-3 with no central 10, the 10's work is usually done by the two 8s and the winger who cuts inside.

What skills does a number 10 need?

A number 10 needs a clean first touch on the half-turn, passing with both feet, the ability to protect the ball in tight spaces, a shot from 16–20 meters, and above all fast, good decisions. Technique gets you the ball between the lines; decisions decide what happens next.

  • Receiving on the half-turn: body open, ball on the back foot, first touch forward.
  • Protecting the ball: body between defender and ball before you try anything.
  • Passing: firm, inside of the foot, both feet, one or two touches; through balls, one-twos, cut-backs, switches.
  • Shooting from distance: low and accurate from the edge of the box, released quickly.
  • Scanning: knowing what is behind you before the ball arrives (see how to scan in soccer).

How does a number 10 decide? 5 rules

A good number 10 decides before the ball arrives: find a gap between the lines, scan to see who is behind you, and know already whether you will turn, set it back or play first time. Facing forward, you pick the through ball, the switch or the shot based on what the defense gives you.

1. Find the pocket, then keep moving

A pocket is a gap between their midfield and defense where no defender can step to you without leaving his job. Stand in the passing lane, not behind an opponent. If the ball isn't coming, move: drift to the blind side of their holding midfielder, or drop deeper if their lines are squeezed together.

2. Scan twice before you receive

Look over both shoulders while your teammate is preparing the pass and once while it travels. You are looking for one thing first: how close is the nearest defender, and from which side.

3. Turn only when the scan says yes

  • Nobody within about 3–4 meters behind you: open up and turn.
  • Defender tight, and he is on one shoulder: roll him on the other side.
  • Defender tight and square behind you: set it back first time to a teammate facing forward, and spin off for the return.

4. Pick the final pass by what the defense gives you

What you seeBest option
A runner and a gap between center back and full-backThrough ball into the space in front of him
Their back line deep, a clear lane to goalShoot from the edge of the box
Everyone shifted to your side, far winger aloneSwitch it yourself
A defender steps out to youPlay into the space he left

5. Press on triggers, not all the time

Don't chase every pass. Go hard on a trigger: a pass to a player facing his own goal, a bad touch, a back pass, a pass towards the touchline. The rest of the time, block the lane into their holding midfielder.

3 situations: what would you do as the number 10?

Decide before you read the answer. You are the lime player; their goal is at the top.

Situation 1: back to goal between the lines

It is 0-0 after 30 minutes. Your left midfielder plays it into you between their lines. You have your back to their goal.

What do you do?

  • A Turn and drive at their back line.
  • B Set it back first time to your right midfielder. (pro pick)
  • C Take a touch and shield it.

Why B: Their DM is tight on your back. Your right midfielder is free, facing forward, nobody within 10 meters. One touch back and he plays forward with time while their DM is stuck behind you. Spin off for the return.

Why not A: Turning is right when nobody is behind you. Here you turn straight into their DM.

Why not C: Shielding is the call when no teammate is free. Here one is, and every second you hold it their other midfielder gets closer.

Situation 2: the final pass

It is 0-0 after 62 minutes. You have the ball facing forward, 25 meters out in the left half-space. Your striker and both wingers are up, and your left winger is starting a run.

What do you do?

  • A Shoot from 25 meters.
  • B Switch it to your right winger.
  • C Slide it into the channel for your left winger's run. (pro pick)

Why C: The gap between their right full-back and center back is more than 12 meters, and your winger is running into it from behind the line, so he is onside. Play it into the space in front of him, not to his feet.

Why not A: A shot is a good answer when the lane is clear. Here their center back stands right in it.

Why not B: The switch is right when the far full-back has tucked in. Here he is about five meters from your winger, so the ball travels a long way and the 1v1 is still waiting.

Situation 3: pressing as a 10

It is 0-0 after 20 minutes. They are building from the back. Their center back passes into their midfielder in front of you.

What do you do?

  • A Press him from behind as the ball arrives. (pro pick)
  • B Step across to their other midfielder.
  • C Drop back to protect the space behind you.

Why A: A pressing trigger. He is coming towards his own center back, so he receives facing his own goal. Your left midfielder closes one side and your striker covers their DM, so there is no easy bounce pass. Arrive with the ball and he goes backwards or loses it.

Why not B: Your right midfielder is already on that player. Leave and you give the man on the ball time to turn into the space you left.

Why not C: Right when the receiver is facing forward. Here dropping gives him time to turn instead of forcing him backwards.

How do you train as a number 10 alone?

You can train most of the number 10's game alone with a wall, a ball and a few cones: receiving on the half-turn, both-foot passing, tight control and shooting from the edge of the box. Add one video session a week for decisions. Three or four sessions of 30–45 minutes fits next to team training.

How do you analyze a number 10 on TV?

To analyze a number 10 on TV, watch the player, not the ball, for one half. Every time he receives between the lines, pause, decide what you would do, then press play and compare with what the pro did.

  1. Before he gets it: where does he stand, and does he scan?
  2. When it arrives: pause. Turn, set back, first time or shoot? Say it out loud.
  3. Press play: what did he do, and what did he see that you didn't?
  4. Without the ball: when does he press, and when does he just block a lane?
  5. Write down one thing to copy in your next game.

What are the most common number 10 mistakes?

The most common number 10 mistakes are standing still between the lines, receiving without scanning, dribbling into a crowded middle, holding the ball until the lane closes, and switching off after losing it. Almost all are decisions, not technique.

  • Hiding behind a defender. If he is between you and the ball, you aren't available. Move a few meters.
  • Receiving square and blind. Scan, then open up so you can see the field.
  • Dribbling into traffic. In a crowded middle, a one-two beats a dribble.
  • Releasing too late. The best pass is the one you play while it is still open.
  • Being predictable. Always cutting onto one foot is easy to defend.
  • Switching off without the ball. Press on triggers and block the lane to their midfield.

FAQ

What does number 10 mean in soccer?

It means the attacking midfielder who plays behind the striker and creates chances, and the shirt number that player traditionally wears. The name comes from the 2-3-5 era, when the inside left forward wore 10.

Is a number 10 a midfielder or a forward?

A midfielder by position, the most advanced one. But the job is mostly in the final third, so a 10 is judged like a forward: chances created, assists and goals.

What is the difference between a number 8 and a number 10?

An 8 covers more ground, box to box, and helps defend and build up. A 10 plays higher, between the lines, and focuses on the final pass and shots. In a 4-3-3, the two 8s often share the 10's job.

Who is the best number 10 of all time?

There is no official answer. Pelé, Maradona, Zidane and Messi are named most often. For learning, pick one whose style fits yours and study how he receives and decides.

What formation uses a number 10?

The 4-2-3-1 uses a central 10 behind one striker, and the 4-3-1-2 and 3-4-1-2 use one behind two strikers. A 4-3-3 usually has no central 10, but its two 8s play the role in the half-spaces.

How do I become a better number 10?

Train receiving on the half-turn, both-foot passing and shooting from the edge of the box, and practise decisions: scan before you receive, choose turn or set back, and pick the final pass by what the defense gives you. Unscouted's free soccer IQ test is a quick way to start.

What does a number 10 do without the ball?

Usually he is the first midfielder in the press. He blocks the pass into their holding midfielder and presses hard on triggers, like a pass to a player facing his own goal or a bad touch.
