Who should pick which

Soccer IntelliGym is probably the better fit if:

  • you like the idea of brain training that adapts its difficulty to you,
  • you're happy to commit to 30-minute sessions two or three times a week,
  • you're training with siblings or a team and can use a family or team plan,
  • you're younger: IntelliGym says it's built for players "as young as 10".

Pause & Decide is probably the better fit if:

  • you want to practise the actual moments you face in a soccer match (or a football match, if that's what you call it),
  • you want the answer explained: what the pro picks and which detail in the picture tells you,
  • you want situations for your own position,
  • you'd rather pay once than keep a subscription running,
  • you have 10 minutes, not 30.

What IntelliGym does well

It's fair to start with the strengths, because there are real ones.

  • It adapts to you. IntelliGym's FAQ says it uses AI to adjust the training levels to each player's strengths and weaknesses. A fixed set of questions can't do that.
  • It goes after the underlying skills. Instead of teaching you what to do in one situation, it aims at the mental skills behind every situation: attention, memory, switching focus.
  • It's built for regular sessions. 30 minutes, two or three times a week, is a clear routine to stick to.
  • Plans for groups. Family plans cover 2 to 4 people, which makes it cheaper per player if siblings or teammates sign up together.
  • A free trial and a refund window. You can try it for 7 days on individual plans.

IntelliGym also says its program is based on long-running research and reports improvement figures from academy studies. Those are the company's own claims. We haven't seen full published details for them, so we're not repeating the numbers here. You can read them on its product page and judge for yourself.

On the App Store, the iOS app had 2.5 stars from 22 ratings when we checked. That's a small number of ratings, so don't read too much into it either way.

Where a scenario trainer is different

IntelliGym's FAQ says the program has "no fantasy stories or complex graphics" and that it "simulates the cognitive demands" of a game. Pause & Decide takes the other route: it shows you the match moment itself. Here's one from the striker set.

  • A Shoot at the near post.
  • B Square it to your winger at the back post.
  • C Cut it back to your midfielder arriving at the penalty spot. (pro pick)

Why C: their midfielder is still behind your runner, and both center backs have dropped towards goal, so the space around the penalty spot is empty. Your teammate can shoot first time, facing goal. A shot is never a bad idea in the box, but their keeper is on the near post with a center back in the way. The pass to the back post has to beat the keeper and a defender, and their full-back is right on your winger.

That's the whole format: a real picture, 15 seconds, three options a decent player might pick, and an explanation that points at the detail you should have noticed. The differences in practice:

  • Real moments: you're practising the exact pictures you'll see on Saturday, not abstract tasks.
  • Position-specific: a winger and a center back face very different decisions. You get 20 situations for each position.
  • Short: 10 situations take a few minutes. Easy to do on the bus.
  • One payment: $27 and it's yours. No renewals to remember.

And the honest limits: Pause & Decide does not adapt to you, it doesn't train memory or peripheral attention as separate skills, and 100 situations is a fixed set, so once you've been through them you're repeating them. We also don't have studies of our own to point to. If adaptive brain training is what you want, that's IntelliGym's strength.

Try before you buy

Both products let you test them first, in different ways:

  • IntelliGym: a 7-day free trial on individual plans, plus a money-back window (7 days on monthly, 30 days on prepaid plans). Read the terms on its pricing page.
  • Pause & Decide: the free soccer IQ test uses the same format: 8 situations for your position, 15 seconds each, with the pro pick and why after every answer. No email or card needed to play.

A simple way to decide: do the free test. If you liked reading real match pictures and the explanations helped, the 100-situation version will suit you. If you found yourself wanting longer, adaptive sessions that work on attention and memory, try IntelliGym's trial.

FAQ

How much does Soccer IntelliGym cost?

Family plans for 2 to 4 people are $17.90, $14.95 or $12.90 per month. On the App Store the prices are $21.90 monthly, $99.90 seasonal and $190.99 annual. Check the official page for current prices.

Does IntelliGym have a free trial?

Yes. Its pricing page offers a 7-day free trial on individual plans. It also lists a 7-day money-back guarantee on monthly plans and 30 days on 6-month and 12-month plans.

Is IntelliGym worth it?

It depends on what you want. If you want adaptive cognitive training sessions and will stick to 30 minutes two or three times a week, it may suit you, and the free trial lets you find out. If you'd rather practise real match situations for your position in short sessions, without a subscription, a scenario trainer like Pause & Decide is a closer fit.

What age is IntelliGym for?

IntelliGym's product page says it's for players "as young as 10 years old to 25 and over". Pause & Decide is written for players aged 15 to 21.

Is there a cheaper alternative to IntelliGym?

Pause & Decide costs $27 once, which is about the same as one to two months of an IntelliGym individual plan. Small-sided games with friends are free. The book Soccer iQ was around $9.99 per volume in 2021. They train decisions in different ways, so compare the format as well as the price.
