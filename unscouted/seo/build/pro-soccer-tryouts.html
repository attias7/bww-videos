How do pro soccer tryouts work?

A professional soccer tryout (called a "trial" in most football countries) is an open event where any eligible player can register, pay a fee and play in front of a pro club's coaching staff. It is the first filter. Players who stand out are invited to a smaller, invite-only combine or to preseason training, and only after that might a club offer a contract.

The usual path looks like this:

  1. Open tryout. Usually one day, mostly matches.
  2. Combine or second session. Invite only. San Jose's MLS NEXT Pro side, for example, invited selected players to a December combine.
  3. Preseason trial. You train with the first team. Sacramento Republic FC guarantees at least one tryout player an invitation to preseason camp.
  4. Contract, or not. Some clubs sign players this way. Chattanooga FC's head coach said, "We have signed a player from the open tryout every year that I've been here." Sacramento says six players have signed with its first team after tryouts since 2014.

Those are real outcomes, but they are a handful of players out of many. Treat a tryout as a chance to be seen and to measure yourself, not as a likely contract.

Who can try out for a pro soccer team?

Most US open tryouts are for players aged 16 to 25, but each club sets its own limits. In the tryouts we checked, minimum ages were 16 (Sacramento, Snohomish), 17 (Brooklyn) and 18 (San Jose, which capped it at 25). Under-18s often need a parent or guardian to sign a waiver.

  • Register the right position. San Jose required players to play the position they registered.
  • College players: trying out with a pro team, or having a club pay your costs, can affect NCAA eligibility. Ask your school's compliance office before you sign up.
  • You pay your own way. At the tryouts that mentioned it, travel, food and hotel were on the player.

How should you prepare in the 8 weeks before a tryout?

Use the 8 weeks to get match fit for two full games in one day, sharpen your first touch and scanning, and practise making quick, simple decisions in your position. Don't try to learn new skills. Get reliable at what you already do, then taper in the last week so you arrive fresh.

1BaselineRegister early. Film a full game. Take the free decision test. List your 2 strengths and 1 weakness.
2Fitness baseTwo interval runs (for example 10 x 30 seconds hard, 30 seconds easy). Two solo touch sessions.
3First touch and scanningWall passes on the half-turn. Look over your shoulder before every touch. Count it in your next game.

5Decisions under pressure20 minutes of freeze-frame match situations, 3 times this week. Pick the option, then check why.
6Game loadPlay two games in a weekend, or one game plus a long session, to copy tryout day.
7SharpenShort, fast sessions. Finishing or crossing reps for your role. Plan travel, kit and food.
8TaperCut volume by about half. Sleep, hydrate, one light touch session two days before.

If you're coming back from injury or haven't played full games recently, start the fitness work earlier. Two full matches in one day is a lot if you're not used to it.

What happens on tryout day, and what drills should you expect?

Expect check-in, a short warm-up, maybe a few drills, then most of the day in 11v11 matches. Spokane's USL League One tryout gave each player two 60-minute matches, and San Jose's MLS NEXT Pro tryout ran two 50-minute matches with a long rest between. Clubs judge you in games far more than in drills.

Common soccer tryout drills, if a club uses them before the games:

  • Rondos (for example 5v2): quick passing, body shape, scanning.
  • Passing patterns: clean, firm passes with both feet.
  • 1v1s: attacking and defending in tight space.
  • Small-sided games (4v4 to 7v7): lots of touches, decisions under pressure.
  • Finishing and crossing: mostly for forwards and wide players.
  • Goalkeeper sessions: handling, distribution, crosses. Spokane added a separate 40-minute GK session.

What to bring: boots for firm and soft ground, shin guards, two water bottles, food for the break, a printed or phone copy of your registration. Arrive early: Chattanooga's check-in was an hour before the first session.

What do coaches watch for at pro tryouts?

Coaches watch whether you make the right decision quickly, check your surroundings before receiving, work hard without the ball and talk to teammates you met that morning. Brooklyn FC's staff said they assess technical ability, tactical understanding, physical readiness and professionalism. Tricks matter far less than doing simple things well and fast.

What mistakes should you avoid at a soccer tryout?

The biggest mistakes are trying to do too much, hiding when the game gets hard and arriving unfit for two full matches. Coaches have a short time to judge many players, so a few lost balls from forcing tricks can outweigh one great moment.

  • Forcing highlight moments. Keep it simple until you've earned the ball and the picture is clear.
  • Playing out of position. Register your best position and play it properly.
  • Going quiet. Silent players look lost in a team of strangers.
  • Sulking after a mistake. Coaches watch your next five seconds.
  • Peaking in the first game. Save energy for the second match. It often matters more.
  • Doing one tryout and stopping. Ask for feedback if you can, then go again next season.

How do you spot a pay-to-try soccer scam?

A registration fee paid on a club's official website is normal for US open tryouts. The red flags are being asked to pay a person, an "agent" or a third party for a private trial, promises of guaranteed contracts or scouts, and invitations that come through social media instead of the club. Check every offer with the club directly.

  • Paying an agent for a trial. FIFPRO, the global players' union, warns that "a club will never ask you to pay for a trial" arranged through an agent, and that a real agent is paid only after you sign a contract.
  • Invitation by DM or personal email. A real trial invite comes from the club's official email. FIFPRO advises checking it with the club.
  • Guarantees. Real clubs don't promise anything: the San Jose and Brooklyn tryout pages both say selection isn't guaranteed.
  • Not on the club's site. If the event isn't on the club's official website or social accounts, assume it isn't real.
  • "European trial" packages. FIFA rules ban international transfers of players under 18, with narrow exceptions. Be very careful with paid offers to go abroad.

FAQ

Do MLS teams hold open tryouts?

Most open tryouts linked to MLS clubs are for their MLS NEXT Pro second teams, like San Jose's The Town FC. Check each MLS club's official website and its second team's site for current events.

How much do professional soccer tryouts cost?

On the official club pages we checked in October 2026, fees ranged from $40 (a USL League Two club) to $225 (an MLS NEXT Pro club). Travel, food and hotel are extra and on you.

How old do you have to be for USL soccer tryouts?

It depends on the club. Sacramento Republic FC (USL Championship) accepted players 16 and older, and Brooklyn FC 17 and older. Under-18s may need a parent's waiver.

What drills are at soccer tryouts?

Pro open tryouts are mostly 11v11 matches. Before the games, clubs may use rondos, passing patterns, 1v1s, small-sided games and finishing, plus a separate session for goalkeepers.

Can you get a contract from an open tryout?

It happens, but rarely. Sacramento Republic FC says six players have signed with its first team after tryouts since 2014. Usually the next step is an invitation to a combine or preseason.

When are pro soccer tryouts held?

The ones we checked ran between November and April, before the season starts. Dates change every year, so check the club's official website in early autumn.

Is paying for a soccer tryout a scam?

Not if you register on the club's official website: most of the US open tryouts we checked charged a fee. Paying an individual or agent for a private trial, or being promised a contract, are red flags.
