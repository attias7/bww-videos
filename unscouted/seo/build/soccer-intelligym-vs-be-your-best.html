Quick answer: Soccer IntelliGym is screen-based cognitive training: 30-minute sessions on a computer or phone that work on awareness, memory and decisions, from $15.90 to $21.90 a month for one player. Be Your Best is VR training: you stand in a virtual match and physically scan around you, for $19 a month billed yearly or $29 month to month, with 3 seats. Pick IntelliGym if you have no headset and want structured brain-training sessions. Pick Be Your Best if you have (or can borrow) a Meta Quest and want to build the head-turning scanning habit. If you don't want a subscription at all, there's a third, cheaper route at the end.

The short version: who should pick which

Soccer IntelliGym is probably the better fit if:

  • you don't own a VR headset and don't want to buy one,
  • you like structured sessions: IntelliGym recommends 30 minutes, 2 to 3 times a week,
  • you want a free trial before paying: individual plans have a 7-day trial,
  • you're younger: IntelliGym says it's for players "as young as 10".

Be Your Best is probably the better fit if:

  • you already have a Meta Quest, or your club or a friend has one you can log into,
  • your main problem is not checking your shoulder before you receive the ball,
  • you want to feel like you're in a match: moving players, 11-a-side, your head and body turning,
  • you can share the cost: the personal plan includes 3 seats.

Both are subscriptions. Both target "soccer IQ" (or football IQ, depending on where you live), but they go at it from opposite ends: IntelliGym trains the thinking behind the picture, Be Your Best trains getting the picture in the first place.

How they train you differently

A bad decision on the ball usually has one of two causes. Either you didn't see something (the free man, the defender coming from your blind side), or you saw it and didn't know what it meant. The two products lean towards different halves of that.

IntelliGym: training the processing

IntelliGym doesn't try to look like a soccer match. Its FAQ says it is "not a video game" and skips "fantasy stories or complex graphics" on purpose. The idea is to train general mental skills that sit underneath every match situation: keeping track of several things at once, switching attention, noticing things at the edge of your vision. Its FAQ also says the software adjusts the difficulty to each player's strengths, weaknesses and pace.

What that means for you: you sit at a screen and do focused sessions. You won't be practising "what do I do when their full-back steps up", you'll be practising the attention and memory that help you notice it. Some players love that. Others find it hard to connect to Saturday's game.

Be Your Best: training the looking

Be Your Best puts you inside a match. Players move, the ball travels, and you have to turn your head to see what's behind you, just like on the field. The modes run from short challenges up to 11-a-side matchplay against AI and a career mode, with stats on your scanning.

What that means for you: it is the closest thing to building the physical habit of checking your shoulder without being on a field. The trade-off is the hardware. You need a headset and some space to move, and a session is not something you can do on the bus.

A simple test: think about your last match. If you mostly got caught on the ball by someone you never saw, scanning is your bigger problem and VR targets it directly. If you saw the options but froze or picked the wrong one, the processing and decision side is your bigger problem.

The research claims, read carefully

Both companies lead with numbers, so it is worth knowing where they come from.

  • IntelliGym says its technology started as a cognitive simulator for fighter pilots, and its "why IntelliGym" page reports a 27% greater on-field improvement in a study with PSV Eindhoven and AZ Alkmaar academies, compared with players who only watched video clips. These are the company's own figures.
  • Be Your Best says players improve their scan rate by 28% on average over 9 weeks, citing a study with a German youth national team. It also cites research by Jordet, Bloomfield and Heijmerikx (2013) linking higher scan rates to more successful forward passes.

We haven't read the full studies behind either company's headline number, so we're reporting them as their claims, not as checked facts. A fair reading: both approaches point to some research, the studies they describe were run with academy or national-team youth players rather than players training alone, and neither number tells you how much you will improve. A trial or money-back window is a better test than any percentage.

Check these before you pay

We found a few things on both sites that don't quite line up. None of them is a red flag on its own, but ask before you commit:

  • IntelliGym's annual total. The pricing page shows "$262/12 Monthly Plan" next to the $15.90/mo annual plan. $15.90 × 12 is about $191, which matches the App Store's $190.99. The $262 looks like the monthly plan's yearly cost shown for comparison, but check the total at checkout.
  • IntelliGym on phone vs computer. The pricing page lists "Full access to computer software" only on the annual plans. If you want to train on a computer, confirm your plan includes it.
  • Be Your Best's minimum term. The pricing page offers $29 billed monthly, but the FAQ says "the minimum subscription length is 12 months". The FAQ may be out of date. Ask before you sign up for monthly.
  • Be Your Best's headset list. The FAQ lists Oculus Quest 1 and 2 and Pico Neo 2, while the app is sold on the Meta Quest store. Check your exact headset is supported.

FAQ

Is Soccer IntelliGym or Be Your Best better?

Neither is better for everyone. IntelliGym suits players without a VR headset who want structured, screen-based cognitive sessions, and it has a 7-day free trial. Be Your Best suits players with access to a Meta Quest who mainly want to build the scanning habit in a moving, match-like scene. Pick based on your hardware, your budget and whether scanning or deciding is your bigger weakness.

Which is cheaper, IntelliGym or Be Your Best?

For one player with no headset, IntelliGym is cheaper: about $191 a year on its 12-month plan, against $228 a year for Be Your Best's annual plan plus the cost of a headset. If three people share Be Your Best's 3 seats, it works out at about $76 each per year. Prices checked October 2026.

Do I need a VR headset for Be Your Best?

For the VR training, yes. The app is on the Meta Quest store. Its FAQ says you can use your account on a headset belonging to a friend, coach or club, and mentions a PC version that runs in Chrome. The phone app is a companion for progress and stats, not the VR training itself.

Does either one have a free trial?

IntelliGym offers a 7-day free trial on individual plans. Be Your Best doesn't mention a free trial but offers a 14-day money-back guarantee.
